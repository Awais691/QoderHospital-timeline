Getting ready

Before your visit.

A little preparation helps appointments run smoothly and gives you more time to talk about what matters.

Bring with you

  • Photo identification
  • Insurance or payment details, if relevant
  • A current medication list, including supplements
  • Referral letter and recent results
  • Names and doses of any allergies

Make the most of it

  • Write down your top two or three questions
  • Note when symptoms started and what changes them
  • Bring someone you trust if that helps
  • Ask for an interpreter if you need one
  • Arrive 10–15 minutes early for check-in

Medication questions

Do not stop or change a prescribed medicine before an appointment unless a clinician has told you to. If you are unsure, call the clinic or your pharmacist.

Investigations

Tests and preparation.

Some tests need planning. If you are unsure whether to eat, drink or take your usual medicines, contact the clinic before the appointment.

Blood tests

Most blood tests do not require fasting. If a fasting test has been requested, follow the written instructions from your clinician. Drink water unless told otherwise.

Imaging (X-ray, CT, MRI, ultrasound)

Tell the imaging team if you are pregnant or might be, have a pacemaker or metal implant, or have had a reaction to contrast dye. Some scans require you to avoid food for a few hours beforehand.

Cardiac tests

For monitoring or stress tests, you may be asked to avoid caffeine or certain medicines. The cardiac team will give specific instructions based on the test requested.

Results

Results are reviewed by the clinician who requested the test. Ask before you leave when to expect them and how you will be contacted. Urgent results are communicated directly to the clinical team.

Surgery

Preparing for surgery.

Planned surgery includes an assessment before the day, clear instructions and a recovery plan that starts before you are admitted.

Before the day

  • Attend your pre-operative assessment
  • Follow fasting instructions exactly
  • Ask which medicines to take or pause
  • Arrange transport home and help for the first days

After surgery

  • Follow wound-care and medication instructions
  • Start gentle movement as advised
  • Know who to contact if you are worried
  • Attend your follow-up review

This is general sample information, not personal medical advice. Always follow instructions from your own clinical team.

Children

Visiting with children.

Children are welcome, with a little planning. Our fictional children's centre has a separate waiting and play area, and staff who are used to helping families feel at ease.

  • Bring a familiar toy, blanket or book for comfort.
  • Explain what will happen in simple, honest words.
  • Tell staff what helps your child settle.
  • Keep children supervised in clinical areas.
  • Ask for a pause if your child becomes distressed.

A note for parents: you do not need to be an expert in the treatment to be an expert in your child. Your knowledge of their signals and routines is valuable clinical information.

Maternity

Maternity information.

Our fictional maternity centre supports pregnancy, birth and the first days with a new baby. Care is shared between midwives, obstetricians and neonatal teams.

What to pack

  • Comfortable clothes and toiletries
  • Baby clothes, nappies and a blanket
  • Medication list and maternity notes
  • Phone charger and snacks

When to call

  • Regular painful contractions before your due date
  • Reduced baby movements
  • Bleeding, severe headache or visual changes
  • Your waters breaking

This is general sample information only. For concerns in pregnancy, contact your own maternity team or emergency services.

Accessibility

Accessibility and support.

Care should be usable by everyone. Our fictional hospital plans for physical, sensory, cognitive and language needs at every stage of a visit.

Getting around

  • Accessible bathrooms on every patient floor
  • Wheelchairs available at the main entrance
  • Accessible parking and drop-off

Communication

  • Interpreters by phone or in person
  • Hearing loops at reception and key clinics
  • Large-print information on request

Costs

Billing and insurance.

In a real hospital, costs depend on your insurance, the care provided and where you are treated. This demonstration does not charge for anything and does not collect payment details.

Before care

Ask whether a service is covered, what your expected contribution might be, and whether financial assistance is available.

After care

Check the itemised bill, compare it with your insurance explanation of benefits, and ask about anything unclear.

Records

Medical records and privacy.

You have a right to understand how your information is used and to ask for access to your records. In a real service, requests usually go through a dedicated health information team.

  • Ask what information is recorded and who can see it.
  • Request a copy of your records through the official process.
  • Tell us if any information is incorrect.
  • Ask for an interpreter or accessible format if needed.

Read our privacy policy for how this demonstration handles (or rather, does not collect) information.

Respect & rights

Your rights and responsibilities.

Care works best when expectations are clear on both sides. These are the principles our fictional hospital would commit to.

You can expect

  • Respect, dignity and privacy
  • Clear information about your care
  • To be involved in decisions
  • Accessible communication support
  • A way to raise concerns without fear

We ask that you

  • Share information that affects your care
  • Treat staff and other patients with respect
  • Follow agreed care plans where you can
  • Attend or cancel appointments promptly
  • Tell us when something is not right
